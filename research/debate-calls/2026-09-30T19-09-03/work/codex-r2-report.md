## New sustained-recovery test

There are 24 configurations, each with 57-253 fit trades. Positive fit means: 0; positive fit trimmed means: 0. These overlapping configurations are not independent statistical trials.

The rule waits at least 15 or 60 minutes, then compares adjacent 5- or 15-minute windows of complete one-minute bars. Recent average close must exceed prior average close by 10%; recent volume must be at least 1x or 2x prior volume; current close must exceed recent volume-weighted close and retain at least half the initial call price. Reported liquidity must be at least $1,000. Search ends at 180 minutes. Exits: +50%/-30%; +1100%/-80%; or trail armed at +100%, 50% trail, -50% initial stop. Every trade is 0.1 SOL. Entries are decided at the observed bar close and filled at the next open.

## Rebuttal: negative net is not universally negative gross

The completed-pre-call 15-minute run-up upper-quartile threshold, learned on fit, is 5.583937847x. Enter at the call and use +50%/-30%. It reproduces Claude's best reported filter (fit n=145, validation n=93). The gross counterfactual replays identical entries, policy fills and horizon marks with zero costs; it does not assume different exit ordering or improved execution.

Therefore the claim that this filter loses before costs is contradicted by both research splits. These are exploratory positive gross samples, not proof of predictive power or a deployable edge. Its gross trimmed means are below the 0.006 SOL fixed round-trip charge alone: subtracting just that charge gives -0.00005 SOL on fit and -0.00171 SOL on validation, before venue fees and impact. Those costs must remain in any scored proposal.

## Submission and concessions

Claude R2 and Codex R2 both abstain: n=0, total SOL=0, total without best=0, captured5x/captured10x=0/0 on each split; mean, trimmed mean and win rate are undefined. There are no trades, caller exposures, weeks or winners carrying either proposal. The zero outcome is structural, not an estimated trading effect; the minimum-sample rule prevents treating abstention as measured alpha. The two no-entry functions must tie under the referee on any holdout.

I concede Claude's deployment decision and its correction that a tie does not indicate trading skill. I dispute the universal negative-gross claim, and the assertion that these finite, overlapping tests refute every possible price/volume strategy. The evidence supports no demonstrated profitable rule at the specified costs. Validation has already been inspected in previous debate rounds; it is not an untouched holdout. New-rule ranking here used fit only, but the overall research process remains exploratory.

No unseen-call advantage over Claude R2 is claimed. Retaining cash avoids the demonstrated net losses; it can underperform any genuinely profitable future strategy. A later active proposal would need positive after-cost trimmed results with at least 30 trades in each research split, no dependence on one caller/week, and a separate sealed evaluation. No automatic untested entry gate has been added.
